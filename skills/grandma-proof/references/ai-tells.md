# AI tells

A list of what to remove. One tell alone may have a reason; several together show the screen came out on autopilot. For each tell you find: delete it, and only put something back if the screen is worse without it.

## The "GPT landing page"

The most repeated template of all. Do not ship any variation of it:

- a giant centered headline filling the whole screen;
- a punchy line instead of saying what the product does;
- one word of the headline in italics or in an "elegant" serif;
- a pill badge above the headline ("New", "AI-powered", "Beta");
- a light gray paragraph below that repeats the headline with more words;
- two buttons side by side ("Get started" and "Learn more");
- three identical cards with an icon, a title and one sentence;
- a "trusted by" logo strip;
- sections in the usual order: features, testimonials, pricing, FAQ, final call to action.

**Do this instead:** a left-aligned headline of normal size that says what the product does in plain words. Beside it or right below, the real product (the screen, the photo, the example). One primary button. Sections chosen by what this product needs to explain.

## Color and effects

- purple, violet or blue-violet gradients;
- gradient-painted text;
- neon glow and colored shadows around buttons or cards;
- frosted glass (blurred translucent background) for no reason;
- blurred color blobs in the background;
- dark background with a decorative grid or dots;
- animated gradient borders.

**Do this instead:** one accent color, used on the main action. Flat background. A quiet gray shadow, only where there is a real layer (an open menu, a dialog on top).

## Shape and layout

- cards inside cards inside cards;
- everything with very round corners, all the same;
- everything centered, including long text;
- an icon in a colored square before every title;
- emoji used as icons or at the start of titles and buttons;
- big decorative numbers ("01", "02", "03");
- small uppercase letter-spaced labels above every section;
- different gaps between similar blocks;
- animation on everything that enters the screen.

**Do this instead:** bring similar things closer and push different things apart, rather than adding frames. Long text left-aligned. Icons from one family. Animation only to show that something changed.

## Copy

- sales verbs: "Transform", "Elevate", "Revolutionize", "Supercharge", "Unlock";
- vague promises: "effortless", "seamless", "intelligent", "end-to-end";
- staged contrast: "It's not just X. It's Y.";
- word trios: "Fast. Simple. Powerful.";
- a dash in every sentence;
- rhetorical question headlines ("Tired of spreadsheets?");
- "Welcome back!" and exclamation marks everywhere;
- forgotten placeholder text ("Lorem ipsum", "John Doe", "Acme Inc").

**Do this instead:** state the fact. "Send an invoice in 2 minutes" rather than "Transform your billing workflow".

## Invented proof

Never create, not even as a temporary example:

- numbers ("10,000+ customers", "99.9%");
- testimonials and people's names;
- client logos;
- ratings and stars;
- security or award badges.

If there is no real data, leave it out. Tell the user the slot is ready for the real data.

## What is not a tell

Do not delete everything in sight. Strong color, unusual type, gradients and animation are good when they have a reason tied to the product. The visual identity already in the project stays as it is. The problem is the choice made on autopilot, not the bold choice.
